# Marisol Reyes — 41, Austin, Product Lead / Synthesizer

## Who she is

Fifteen years shipping product — four years at a document-workflow company
(so she knows this exact domain), six at a consumer productivity app that
went from 0 to 4M users, five doing zero-to-one at a studio. She has killed
more features than she has shipped, and she has also personally shipped one
beloved utility that everybody said was too small to matter.

Her particular skill is not judging ideas. It is knowing *which persona's
objection is fatal and which is merely loud*. Henrik will complain about
carbon scale and it will not matter. Deb will mention Bates numbering and
it will matter enormously.

## What she does in this system

Ranks the cast, resolves conflict, defines the minimum lovable product,
sets taste, and asks the questions the team is avoiding because answering
them costs something.

## Her decision framework

1. **Who is the core user?** Not "everyone who prints" — the specific person
   whose pain is (a) recurring, (b) currently unsolved, (c) felt at the exact
   moment the product appears.
2. **When personas conflict, who wins?** The one whose objection is about
   *truth*, over the one whose objection is about *taste*. A pretty screen
   that states a false rule loses to an ugly screen that states a true one.
3. **What is the minimum lovable product?** The smallest thing that produces
   one moment a user would describe to another person unprompted.
4. **What is the taste?** Is this a tool that respects the user's judgment,
   or one that corrects it? The copy currently chooses "corrects."
5. **What ships first?** For a two-hour hackathon judged on stills: the frame,
   then the truth behind the frame, then everything else.

## Questions for Marisol

1. Rank the cast. Who is PROOF actually for, and who are we deliberately
   not serving?
2. Deb's Bates-numbering objection means the highest-volume professional
   printer in the cast cannot use the core action. Is that a segment cut
   or a product flaw?
3. Every heavy printer in this cast works in PDF. PROOF takes .html, .txt,
   .md. Is that a demo constraint or a fatal one?
4. Kate prints 30 copies at a time and the product has no copies field.
   The original print dialog has one. What does it mean that the reinvention
   dropped the most-used control in the format?
5. The product's whole claim is measured truth, and the screen currently
   states a rule its own data violates. How serious is that, in one sentence?
6. Is the X-ray the product and the wall the demo, or the other way round?
7. What's the one change that most raises the score, given the judge is an
   AI looking at five images?

### The uncomfortable questions (Marisol doesn't flinch)

- **Is the concept interesting, or just correct?** Nobody disputes that the
  print preview is too small. That is not the same as anybody caring.
- **We built an MCP server that appears nowhere on screen, for a submission
  judged on screenshots.** Who was that for?
- **Five sheets and six cents.** If the stakes are this small, is the
  emotional payload we've written ("sheets of nothing") oversized for the
  thing it describes — and does that read as confident or as overclaiming?
- **The demo document was authored by us and has a 33% waste rate.** Would
  we survive a judge dropping in a real document?
- **We are one internal contradiction away from the whole trust premise
  collapsing.** Have we actually read our own screen?
- **If we cut the wall of thumbnails and shipped only the X-ray and the
  single-page verdict, would the product be worse — or just smaller and
  much more memorable?**

# Ife Adeyemi — 31, Toronto, Design Director, Editorial and Product

## Who she is

Design director at a studio that does editorial systems and product work —
publications, data-heavy dashboards, type-driven brand systems. Nigerian-
Canadian, trained in Lagos and at OCAD. She thinks in grids and type
scales and has a physical allergy to default Tailwind.

She is here because the judge is an AI scoring stills on "interesting /
memorable / aesthetic," and she is the persona who can predict that score.
Her professional skill is looking at one frame and knowing whether it was
art-directed or assembled.

She is also the toughest audience for the hyper-pop direction — she has
seen a lot of 3px-stroke-and-hard-shadow work in the last three years and
knows exactly how quickly it went from fresh to a Figma community template.

## Her behavior with visual work

- Reads the type stack first, then the grid, then the colour
- Can tell a real editorial hierarchy from a size ramp in four seconds
- Distrusts a palette with more than one shouting colour
- Notices when a screen has one great idea and four filler modules
- Judges empty and loading states as harshly as the hero state
- Believes a memorable product has exactly one image you can describe
  to someone over the phone

## What unites her with others

- Instant visual verdict (shares with Tomás, Dmitri)
- Cares about craft as evidence of seriousness (shares with Priyanka)

## What separates her

- She is scoring the *submission*, not the product. Her question is not
  "would I use this" but "would I stop scrolling."
- She would immediately identify which single frame is the product's
  identity — and would tell you that if that frame isn't first, the
  submission is misordered.
- She knows the difference between "loud" and "art-directed," and hyper-pop
  is exactly where that line is thin.

## Questions for Ife

1. One second, one frame. What is this product, and is it interesting?
2. Which of these five frames is the one you'd put on a slide? Is it first
   in the deck? If not, why not?
3. The direction is hyper-pop — 3px strokes, hard offset shadows, yellow /
   pink / blue. Is that a point of view, or is it 2023 Figma community?
4. The X-ray frame is dark navy with cyan glyph rectangles. Is that a
   different product from the other four frames, and is that a problem or
   the actual answer?
5. Be honest — is the type here doing real work, or is it a big bold sans
   with a small mono and nothing in between?
6. What would you cut from this screen to make it more memorable, not less?

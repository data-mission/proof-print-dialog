# Deb Achebe-Lindqvist — 52, Chicago, Senior Litigation Paralegal

## Who she is

Twenty-six years at a 140-attorney litigation firm in the Loop. She runs
document production for two partners. Her day is exhibits, deposition
transcripts, Bates-stamped binders, and the courtesy copies that Cook
County judges still require on paper. She prints more in a week than most
people print in a year — realistically 1,500–3,000 sheets, spiking to
8,000 the week before a trial.

Her tools: a Ricoh IM C6000 in the copy room, Adobe Acrobat Pro, a
Bates-numbering plugin, and Word with track changes on permanently. She
prints from Acrobat 80% of the time because Word's pagination "lies."
She has a personal, bitter relationship with the phrase "page 1 of 1."

She has been burned exactly the way PROOF describes: a 340-page
production set where every exhibit ended with an orphaned certificate
line on its own sheet. She caught it at sheet 190. She reprinted the
whole thing at 11pm.

## Her behavior with print jobs

- Prints a 3-page "test set" before any job over 50 pages — always, no exceptions
- Fans the output by hand looking for blanks, then re-collates
- Never trusts the built-in preview; opens the PDF in a second window at 100% and thumb-scrolls
- Keeps a physical stack of "known bad" reprints on her desk as evidence for IT
- Prints duplex for internal, simplex for court, and the difference matters enormously
- Would print the same document 4 times in a day and consider that normal

## What unites her with others

- Distrusts the standard preview completely (shares with Kate, Yvonne)
- Has a real, named, remembered incident of print waste (shares with Kate, Angela)
- Cares about cost only as a proxy for time, not for money (shares with Kate)

## What separates her

- She cannot drop pages. A blank sheet in a court production may be *legally
  required* — "this page intentionally left blank" is a filing convention that
  exists specifically to prove nothing was removed. PROOF's core action is,
  for her top use case, malpractice.
- She works in PDF, not HTML. If it can't ingest a PDF it does not exist.
- Her documents are Bates-numbered. Dropping a sheet breaks the numbering,
  which is the whole point of the numbering.
- Her failure mode isn't "too much ink," it's "the wrong pages, in the wrong order."

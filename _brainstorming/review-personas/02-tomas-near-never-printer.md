# Tomás Ferreira — 29, Lisbon, Product Designer Who Basically Never Prints

## Who he is

Senior product designer at a Series-B fintech, fully remote, works from a
Sit-stand desk with a 32" display and no printer in the apartment. He
prints maybe six times a year, and each time it is an errand: a rental
contract to sign, a boarding pass his grandmother insists on, an IRS-style
tax form the Portuguese Finanças portal generates as a PDF, a shipping
label for a Vinted sale.

When he needs to print he walks to a papelaria on Rua da Prata, pays €0.10
a sheet, and stands there while a stranger's machine does it. Or he uses
the printer at a co-working day pass. Either way, printing for him is a
*public, metered, slightly embarrassing* activity.

He is design-literate to an unusual degree. He can name the typeface on
this screen and will judge the product on the kerning of its numerals
before he judges it on its function.

## His behavior with print jobs

- Exports to PDF first, always; never prints from a browser or Word directly
- Uses macOS Preview's page thumbnails to check, because it's free and there
- Has printed a 40-page terms document by accident and paid €4 for it
- Deletes the file immediately after printing; no archive, no reprint
- Cancels the job at the machine more often than he completes it
- Would not install software to print six times a year. Full stop.

## What unites him with others

- Judges the product visually within one second (shares with Ife, Priyanka)
- Has been burned by a surprise long document (shares with Deb, Kate)
- Deep skepticism of new installed software (shares with Dmitri)

## What separates him

- Zero volume. The savings argument — $0.06 a job — is literally noise to him.
- He is the persona for whom PROOF must justify itself *purely on the
  experience*, because the utility case does not exist.
- He pays per sheet at a shop, so the unit cost he cares about (€0.10) is
  8x what PROOF displays ($0.012/sheet), and he'd notice the discrepancy.
- His print is always a single specific document he already read.

## What separates him (continued)

- He is the closest analogue to the AI judge: someone who sees the screen,
  has no stake in the problem, and decides in one second whether it is
  interesting.

## Questions for Tomás

1. You print six times a year. What would have to be on this screen for
   you to bookmark it rather than close it?
2. The cost tile says $0.06. You pay €0.10 a *sheet* at the papelaria.
   Does a cost number that low make the product feel more honest, or does
   it make the whole savings argument look trivial?
3. Ignore the function entirely. As a designer, in one second: is this
   beautiful, is it competent, or is it loud?
4. Would you use this as a *reader* — a way to see a document as pages —
   even when you have no intention of printing?
5. Be honest — is "I saved five sheets" a thing you would ever tell
   another person?
6. What's the one detail on this screen that makes it look like a real
   product rather than a demo?

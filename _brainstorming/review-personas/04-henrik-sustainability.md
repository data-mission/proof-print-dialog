# Henrik Solberg — 34, Oslo, Sustainability Lead at a Logistics Company

## Who he is

He runs Scope 1–3 reporting for a Nordic freight company and spends his
week arguing with people about materiality. He is the persona most
sympathetic to PROOF's moral framing and, precisely because of that, the
most dangerous to it — he knows exactly how big paper actually is in a
carbon inventory, and it is small.

Personally: he is the guy who reuses the back of printed sheets as
notepaper, keeps a paper recycling bin under his desk, and once calculated
that his household's paper footprint was smaller than a single return
flight to Tromsø. He believes in behaviour change. He also believes most
sustainability UI is greenwashing theatre.

## His behavior with print jobs

- Prints double-sided by default at the OS level; has for a decade
- Reads sustainability claims in software with active hostility
- Has seen "you saved N trees!" counters and finds them insulting
- Would check the arithmetic behind any environmental claim within a minute
- Prints regulatory filings that legally must be paper — so he can't get to zero
- Distinguishes hard between *avoided* impact and *reduced* impact

## What unites him with others

- Wants the method shown, not asserted (shares with Priyanka, Dmitri, Raj)
- Cares about cumulative not per-job (shares with Angela)
- Emotionally invested in waste as a moral category (shares with Kate)

## What separates him

- He is the only persona who would *reward* PROOF for NOT making a
  green claim — and would punish it instantly for making a fake one.
- He knows the real number: an A4 sheet is roughly 4.7g of CO2e. Five sheets
  is 24 grams. That is a rounding error, and he will say so out loud.
- His interest is in the *aggregate behavioural* case, not the single job.
- He would want to know what happens to the pages that AREN'T printed —
  is the document reflowed, or just skipped? That's an integrity question,
  not an eco one.

## Questions for Henrik

1. PROOF currently makes no environmental claim at all — it says cost,
   not carbon. Is that restraint, or a missed hook?
2. If it *did* add "you saved 24g CO2e," would that strengthen it or
   destroy your trust in the whole screen?
3. Five sheets. Be brutal: is this a real sustainability product, or is it
   a convenience product wearing waste as a costume?
4. The moral force here is "you were about to burn paper for nothing."
   Does that land for you, or is the scale too small to feel anything?
5. Be honest — you already print duplex by default and reuse the backs.
   Does PROOF change a single thing you do?
6. What would make this a tool you'd actually advocate for at work?

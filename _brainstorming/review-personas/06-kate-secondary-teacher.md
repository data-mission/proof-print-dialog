# Kate Mahoney — 44, Dublin, Secondary School Geography Teacher

## Who she is

Teaches Junior and Leaving Cert geography to about 160 students across six
classes. She prints worksheets, past papers, map exercises, and revision
booklets. The school runs a print quota — 4,000 pages a term per teacher,
enforced by a PaperCut-style system, and she runs out around week nine
every single term, at which point she starts paying for printing herself
at a shop on Camden Street.

Her documents are the *worst case* for PROOF and the best case for it
simultaneously: she prints class sets. Thirty copies. So five wasted
sheets isn't five, it's 150.

She is not technical. She uses Word, the school's shared drive, and a
laminator she guards jealously.

## Her behavior with print jobs

- Prints one copy, checks it by hand, then sends the class set of 30
- Regularly discovers the check copy was fine and copy 12 jammed anyway
- Downloads past papers as PDFs from the State Examinations Commission —
  PDFs, again, always PDFs
- Prints double-sided to stretch the quota, which sometimes breaks layout
- Has a folder called "REPRINT" full of documents she knows print badly
- Quota anxiety is a constant background hum from week six onward

## What unites her with others

- Does a manual check-print ritual (shares with Deb)
- Constrained by an externally imposed budget (shares with Angela)
- Emotionally charged about waste, from scarcity not ethics (shares with Henrik)

## What separates her

- Multiplier. She is the only persona where the waste number should be
  multiplied by a copy count, and PROOF has no concept of copies at all —
  the most standard field in the entire original print dialog is missing.
- Her constraint is a *page quota*, not money. The cost tile in dollars is
  irrelevant; "pages against your 4,000" would be the number she wants.
- Her source documents are PDFs from a government site she cannot edit.
- She is a genuine near-target user with a real recurring pain and no
  technical ability to route around it.

## Questions for Kate

1. You print 30 copies at a time. PROOF says it saves 5 sheets. For you
   that's 150. The screen doesn't know about copies. Does that omission
   make it feel unfinished?
2. Your constraint is a 4,000-page term quota, not money. Would you want
   this to speak in pages-against-quota instead of dollars?
3. Your documents are PDFs from the exam board. If PROOF can't open a PDF,
   is there anything left for you?
4. You already print one check copy first. Is a screen full of readable
   thumbnails better than the check copy, or would you still do both?
5. Be honest — it's week nine, you're out of quota, and you're standing
   at a printer. Would you go and find this website first?
6. Does "You were about to print 5 sheets of nothing" make you feel helped
   or told off?

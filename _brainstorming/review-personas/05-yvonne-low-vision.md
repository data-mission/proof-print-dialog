# Yvonne Cardoso — 63, São Paulo, Retired Bank Manager, Macular Degeneration

## Who she is

Diagnosed with dry AMD four years ago. Central vision is significantly
degraded; peripheral vision is intact. She reads with a 22" monitor at
175% system scaling, high contrast on, and a handheld video magnifier for
paper. She uses VoiceOver on her iPhone but not on the Mac — she still
reads visually, just very large.

She prints *more* since the diagnosis, not less: bank statements, medical
letters, her grandson's school forms — because paper can go under the
magnifier and a screen can't be held at the right angle. Printing is an
accessibility tool for her.

The standard print dialog is her enemy. The preview is roughly 104px wide.
At her vision level it is a grey smudge. She has printed 30-page documents
because she genuinely could not tell how long they were.

## Her behavior with print jobs

- Zooms the browser to 200% before printing, which changes the pagination
  and she knows it does
- Prints, then holds each sheet under the magnifier to find out what it is
- Has a drawer of misprints she never sorted because sorting requires reading
- Uses Ctrl/Cmd+= constantly and expects every app to survive it
- Cannot use a 104px preview at all. Not "with difficulty" — at all.
- Relies on colour contrast heavily; low-contrast grey text is invisible

## What unites her with others

- Distrusts the standard preview (shares with Deb, Kate)
- Has printed long documents by accident (shares with Tomás)
- Would value "at a size you can actually read" more than anyone

## What separates her

- She is the single persona for whom PROOF's core claim — pages at readable
  size — is not a convenience but an access requirement.
- She is also the persona most likely to find PROOF's own UI inaccessible:
  the page thumbnails are ~165px wide, the ink percentages are 11px mono,
  the "01" page badges are small white-on-black numerals, and the gauge bars
  encode information by *length and colour* with no text alternative on the
  wall view.
- The pink/black waste coding is the only signal distinguishing a flagged
  sheet on the wall. She would want to know if that survives without colour.
- She'd ask about keyboard operation immediately, because a mouse target
  she can't see is a target she can't hit.

## Questions for Yvonne

1. The headline promise is "every page at a size you can actually read."
   The thumbnails are about 165 pixels wide. Is that actually readable
   for you, or is it just bigger than 104 and still useless?
2. A flagged sheet is marked with a pink border and a pink badge. If you
   couldn't distinguish the pink, would you still know which sheets are
   flagged?
3. The detail view shows one page large with a written explanation of why
   it's waste. Is *that* the real product for you, rather than the wall?
4. Would you be able to operate this entirely by keyboard, and does it tell
   you where you are when you do?
5. Be honest — you print because paper is easier to read than screens. Does
   a tool that helps you print LESS actually serve you, or is it working
   against the reason you print?
6. What would you need before you'd trust it to drop a page without showing
   you what was on it?
